A familiar PowerPoint workflow

Draw plays with ordinary PowerPoint text, lines, arrows, circles, and other shapes. The converter renders what is visible inside the field rectangle; it does not need to understand your terminology or your scheme.

The four rules

A deck that follows these basics gives the cropper the clearest possible signal.

  1. Save as .pptxExport older PowerPoint, Keynote, or Google Slides decks to the modern PowerPoint format first.
  2. One play per slideDo not place two fields or multiple play options on the same slide.
  3. Use one large rectangleMake an ordinary, ungrouped PowerPoint rectangle the largest rectangle on every play slide.
  4. Choose one section patternUse no separators for offense-only, or start every section with OFFENSE or DEFENSE in a mixed deck.

No separator slides? That is fine. If the deck contains no OFFENSE or DEFENSE separator, every recognized play slide is treated as offense. For a mixed deck, start the first offense section with OFFENSE and the first defense section with DEFENSE. Once any separator is present, slides before the first separator are ignored.

Anatomy of a reliable play slide

The rectangle defines the crop. Everything important should sit inside it, including the play name.

  1. Play name and numberPut a short, readable label near the top, inside the rectangle.
  2. One explicit field rectangleUse Insert → Shapes → Rectangle and leave it ungrouped. Its object name and PowerPoint language do not matter.
  3. Ordinary visible shapesLines, arrows, freeforms, player circles, and text all render as part of the slide image.
  4. Safe inner marginKeep labels and route tips away from the rectangle edge so cropping and printing do not crowd them.

What works—and what can fail

Renaming a rectangle is safe. Most conversion issues come from how the field object is built, not from the routes themselves.

Grouped field

The field can disappear inside a group. Fix: ungroup it and leave the field rectangle as a top-level shape.

Renamed rectangle: supported

The converter recognizes actual rectangle geometry, even when the object is renamed or PowerPoint uses another language. Keep it ungrouped and top-level.

Missing field shape

A background color or background image is not a rectangle object. Fix: insert a real rectangle behind the play.

Larger decorative rectangle

The cropper uses the largest recognized rectangle. Fix: remove large rectangle borders or make the field clearly largest.

Unsupported file format

The uploader accepts modern PowerPoint files only. Export first; do not upload a link, PDF, Keynote file, or legacy PowerPoint file.

.ppt.key.pdf

Special Office effects

SmartArt, embedded media, unusual fonts, and some ink effects can render differently outside PowerPoint. Fix: use ordinary shapes and common fonts when possible.

Quick check: In PowerPoint, open the Selection Pane and confirm the field is a separate, top-level rectangle object rather than part of a group, table, or background image. If several rectangles exist, make the field clearly the largest.

No LLM

What the converter actually does

The conversion is deterministic, not AI-mediated. It reads the slide’s object structure to locate the large field rectangle, renders the visible slide, and crops that rendering to the rectangle’s bounds.

That is why an ordinary route arrow does not need a special meaning: it only needs to render visibly inside a field the converter can find. It is also why the field rectangle’s object structure matters more than the football notation.

Before you upload

  • The file extension is .pptx.
  • Each play has its own slide.
  • Each play slide has one large, ungrouped rectangle.
  • The field is a real rectangle shape, not a background image or table.
  • The play name and number sit inside the field near the top.
  • A mixed deck starts with the appropriate OFFENSE or DEFENSE separator.
  • No decorative rectangle is larger than the field.
  • Important routes and labels stay clear of the crop edge.
